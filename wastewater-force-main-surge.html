Surge in Wastewater Force Mains: Plastic Pipe, Low Heads, Sewage Air Valves and Thousands of Cycles

Mohamed Abokhatwa | Mechanical Manager · MSc, PMP, PMI-RMP, Envision SP | November 2026 | 14 min read

A wastewater force main looks forgiving: a PE pipe with a wave speed near 270 m/s turns a full stop at 1.84 m/s into a Joukowsky drop of only 50.5 m, a quarter of what ductile iron would see. But force mains run at low pressure, with small pumps and little inertia, and that 50 m is more than the 39 m the pump holds. On our 3 km DN400 example, the pump's real rundown (2.9 kg·m²) changes nothing: the line goes to vapour at the pump. Ten times that inertia still lets the high point at 1.8 km separate. A vessel with 4.3 m³ of gas keeps the line above atmospheric, and then has to live with sewage. This article covers the hydraulics that set wastewater surge apart, the protection options and their trade-offs, the cycle counts that make fatigue matter, and how to model it in HAMMER.

1 · What is different about sewage

  • Plastic pipes and low wave speeds. PE and PVC force mains have wave speeds of 250–450 m/s, and dissolved and entrained gas lowers them further [1][2].
  • Low heads. Force mains lift a few tens of metres, so a moderate downsurge is enough to reach vapour.
  • Small, low-inertia pumps, often submersible, that stop almost at once.
  • Gas. Hydrogen sulfide and other gases collect at high points, and the air valves that release them must be sewage types that will not foul.
  • Solids. They limit what can be put in the line: small orifices clog, and vessels and check valves must tolerate rags and grit.
  • Many cycles. Pumps start and stop on wet-well level, many times an hour, every day.

2 · Wave speed and Joukowsky in plastic pipe

For a thin-walled pipe anchored against axial movement, the wave speed is [1][3]

\[ a \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{K/\rho}{1 + \dfrac{K}{E}\,(SDR-1)}}, \qquad \Delta H = \frac{a\,v}{g} \]

For PE100 at SDR17, with a short-term modulus of 1.2 GPa at about 20 °C, this gives 270 m/s, and a stop from 1.84 m/s gives 50.5 m. The modulus of PE falls with temperature and load duration, so the wave speed in a warm sewer is lower still; take the manufacturer's dynamic modulus, and bracket it (see sensitivity and wave speed, which also covers gas content).

3 · The example force main

A wet well with its water level 2 m above the pump lifts 0.18 m³/s through 3 km of PE100 SDR17 DN400 (353 mm bore) to a discharge manhole whose water level is at 17.0 m. The main rises to 6 m in its first 300 m, to a high point of 12 m at 1.8 km, dips to 9 m and climbs to 16 m at the manhole. Friction at a Darcy factor of 0.015 is 22.0 m, so the pump delivers 37.0 m (93 kW at 1,480 rpm, inertia 2.9 kg·m² from the correlation in pump data). The steady pressure is 39 m at the pump, 13.8 m at the high point and 2.9 m just before the manhole. Every point except the manhole itself sits below the discharge level once the pumps stop, so the line stays full at rest. Because the pressure near the outlet is only 2.9 m in normal running, the design criterion here is no sub-atmospheric pressure (0 m) rather than +3 m; as always, check it against the project specification and the manufacturers' ratings.

Stopping at once, the pump end goes to vapour and the cavity collapse lifts the peak to 54.5 m. The real rundown barely helps: vapour at the pump, a peak of 40.7 m. Ten times the inertia moves the trouble to the high point (vapour at 1.75 km). The vessel, 4.26 m³ of gas growing to 9.34 m³, keeps every point at or above atmospheric, the lowest being the high point.

4 · Protection options for sewage

Surge protection on wastewater force mains: what works and what it costs

In our practice, sewage air valves at the high points with a stronger pipe class are the usual answer on short force mains, and a vessel only where the separation is severe or long-lasting. Vessels on sewage are avoided where possible because of the maintenance they demand, not because they do not work hydraulically [4][5].

5 · Cycles: why fatigue matters on force mains

A wet well controlled on level can start and stop its pumps several times an hour. Each start and each stop is a pressure cycle, and on plastic pipe the number of cycles sets the life (see the pipe's real limits). For PVC the Folkman method gives the cycles to failure from the stress amplitude [6]:

\[ \sigma_{amp} = \frac{(P_{max}-P_{min})(DR-1)}{4}, \qquad N = 10^{\,-4.196\log_{10}\sigma_{amp} + 17.76} \]

For PE the industry approach is to keep the recurring total pressure within 1.5 times the pressure class [7].

Six starts an hour around the clock is 105,000 cycles a year, more than a water transmission main sees in a decade. With the example's unprotected stop (about 4.0 bar at the pump down to vapour), PVC DR18 has a cyclic life of about 107 years with the margin of 2, and PE PN10 is far inside 1.5 × PC. Life falls with roughly the fourth power of the swing: at 10 starts an hour the same pipe gives 64 years, and a 7 bar swing at six starts an hour gives 24.

6 · Modelling it in Bentley HAMMER

Field names differ slightly between versions [8].

  1. Model the wet well as a reservoir or tank at its lowest operating level, which gives the lowest pressures, and repeat at the highest.
  2. Set the wave speed from the pipe's dynamic modulus and SDR with the Wave Speed Calculator, and bracket it for temperature and gas.
  3. Enter the pump's own inertia; submersible sets are often an order of magnitude below dry-well sets of the same power.
  4. Model the discharge manhole as a reservoir at its water level, and check that no part of the main sits above it, or the line will drain at rest.
  5. Add sewage air valves as air valve elements at every high point, with the manufacturer's inflow and outflow orifices, and check that they do not admit air during normal operation.
  6. Run the trip and the normal stop, and export the recurring pressure range at the critical points for the fatigue check.
  7. Check the check valve on each pump against slam with its dynamic characteristic (see check valve slam), since sewage check valves are often slow-closing swing types.

7 · Checklist

  • Wave speed from the dynamic modulus, bracketed for temperature and gas.
  • Pump inertia from the real (often submersible) set.
  • Profile against the discharge level, so the line stays full at rest.
  • Criterion stated and checked against the specification and the manufacturers' ratings.
  • Protection chosen with maintenance in mind: sewage air valves, pipe class, vessel only where needed.
  • Cycles counted and the fatigue checked for plastic pipe.
  • Check valves checked for slam.
